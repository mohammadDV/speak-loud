# German Reflexive Verbs & Pronouns (*Reflexive Verben*) (A2)

Learn when a verb is **reflexive**, choose the right **reflexive pronoun**, place it correctly in the sentence, and know **Accusative vs Dative** forms.

---

## Step 1: What Are Reflexive Verbs?

A **reflexive verb** is used when the person doing the action (the subject) is also the person receiving the action (the object). In English this is often *myself*, *yourself*, or *each other*.

### Reflexive pronouns (Accusative)

**mich**, **dich**, **uns**, and **euch** look like normal accusative pronouns. Only the 3rd person (*er/sie/es/man/sie/Sie*) has a special form: **sich**.

| Subject | Reflexive (Akkusativ) | Example (*sich waschen*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ich** | **mich** | *Ich wasche **mich**.* |
| **du** | **dich** | *Du wäschst **dich**.* |
| **er / sie / es / man** | **sich** | *Er wäscht **sich**.* |
| **wir** | **uns** | *Wir waschen **uns**.* |
| **ihr** | **euch** | *Ihr wascht **euch**.* |
| **sie / Sie** | **sich** | *Sie waschen **sich**.* |

### Types of reflexive verbs

1. **True reflexive actions** — doing something to yourself  
   *sich rasieren*, *sich schminken*, *sich anziehen*  
   Compare: *Sie wäscht **das Baby**.* vs. *Sie wäscht **sich**.*

2. **Reciprocal actions (“each other”)** — two or more people acting on each other  
   *sich küssen*, *sich kennenlernen*, *sich verlieben*

3. **Mandatory reflexive verbs** — the pronoun is part of the verb (not “myself” in English)  
   *sich freuen*, *sich ärgern*, *sich interessieren*, *sich kümmern*

---

## Step 2: Word Order & *Perfekt*

1. **Main clause (*Hauptsatz*):** the reflexive pronoun comes right after the conjugated verb (or right after the subject if the sentence does not start with the subject).  
   *Er wäscht **sich** heute.* / *Heute wäscht er **sich**.*

2. **Subordinate clause (*Nebensatz*):** with *weil*, *dass*, *wenn*… the conjugated verb goes to the end; the reflexive pronoun stays right after the subject.  
   *…weil er **sich** heute geärgert **hat**.*

3. ***Perfekt*:** reflexive verbs **always** use **haben** (never *sein*).  
   *Sie **haben** sich über das Essen geärgert.*

> Note: *heiraten* is **not** reflexive in German (*Sie wollen heiraten* — no *sich*).

---

## Step 3: Accusative vs. Dative Reflexive Pronouns

If the sentence also has a **direct Accusative object** (a body part or thing), the reflexive pronoun switches to the **Dative**.

| No extra object (Akkusativ) | With an Accusative object (Dativ) |
| :--- | :--- |
| *Ich wasche **mich**.* | *Ich wasche **mir** die Hände.* |
| *Zieh **dich** an!* | *Zieh **dir** einen Mantel an!* |

### Only *ich* and *du* change

| Subject | Akkusativ | Dativ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ich** | **mich** | **mir** |
| **du** | **dich** | **dir** |
| **er/sie/es** | sich | sich |
| **wir** | uns | uns |
| **ihr** | euch | euch |
| **sie/Sie** | sich | sich |

### Special meaning: *sich vorstellen*

| Case | Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Akkusativ** | introduce oneself | *Ich möchte **mich** vorstellen.* |
| **Dativ** | imagine something | *Ich kann **mir** das nicht vorstellen.* |

*sich verlieben in* always takes the **Accusative** reflexive: *Er hat **sich** in Anna verliebt.*
